About me

As I begin my first year of Mechanical and Automation Engineering at Chung Chi College, The Chinese University of Hong Kong, I am mapping out the next four years.

Beyond a degree, I want early confidence with CAD, a transcript I can be proud of, industry experience before graduation, and a place for myself on campus. A quadcopter design is already underway in CAD, with breaks reserved to keep it moving.

Doran (1981) wrote his original criteria as Specific, Measurable, Assignable, Realistic and Time-related, while the student version used here (University of Massachusetts Dartmouth, 2024) adapts two of them to Achievable and Relevant. Yemm (2012) adds the feasibility test — time, money, skills, help available — and caps a plan at four key goals. Four follow: three established in Year 1, with certification and internships carrying into years two and three.

The four goals

Short-term · Year 1 → Years 2–3

Goal 01 — SolidWorks certification pathway

Confident CAD skills early means SolidWorks becomes a tool I reach for in design courses and internships, rather than something still being learned when a project requires it. Dedicating eight to ten hours a week will guide me through the phases: sketching and basic extrusions to month three, revolutions and reference geometry to month six, assemblies and drawings to month nine, materials and mass properties to month eleven, timed mocks in month twelve. During exam weeks, study reduces to one maintenance session instead of nothing — and passing earns me an evening to rest, putting Yemm's advice on rewards into practice.

Continued. The same pathway carries on after the CSWA: Certified SolidWorks Professional at month 30, two and a half years from the first session, across three six-month stages — advanced parts, configurations, complex assemblies, one segment each. The closing phase goes to a specialisation (sheet metal, weldments or simulation) and a portfolio project.

SPass the CSWA, then the CSWP plus one specialisation
M80%+ on three mocks, then the official exam; three CSWP segments, one project
AOne fixed session plus weekends, built on CSWA practice
RCore skill and professional credential for mechanical design
TCSWA in 12 months · CSWP by month 30

Short-term · Year 1

Goal 02 — Hold a GPA of 3.5 or above

Since maths and physics underpin mechanical engineering, passing is not enough — understanding is the aim. I will dedicate three to four hours each weekday evening to homework, supplemented with textbook problems, exercise sets and past papers at weekends, and a review every Sunday. Each semester will end with a GPA check: below 3.5 adds one weekly support session for my weakest subject.

Short-term · Year 1 → Year 2

Goal 03 — Campus life, identity & whole-person development

Lectures are only part of university, so campus life is a goal too: a weekend outing with friends at least once a week, and one Chung Chi event each semester. After serving as a student helper at Chung Chi Foundation Day with the Adventure Department, I plan to do it again — and through all of this, I am forming my identity as a Chung Chi student.

Weekends stay open for runs or gym sessions, because physical health supports stamina. Mental health gets equal weight: a break every day, no all-nighters, a Sunday check-in on stress. Quiet weekend time for reading and thinking about why I study keeps perspective — the spiritual side grades cannot measure.

Daily Cantonese practice counts as non-major knowledge and eases joining conversations. In Year 2, I will join PEMICU (the CUHK Indonesian Students' Association) to help incoming Indonesian students adjust while earning hostel points toward a Year 3 dorm room.

Years 1–2

Goal 04 — Gain two internships, starting with research

First-year research placements are rare, but applying costs little as long as a backup is ready. This means ten positions or labs shortlisted by month nine, a CV and referees prepared, five applications submitted before the summer. If nothing lands, a supervised faculty project replaces it. Year 2 then shifts to corporate opportunities — fifteen applications, two career events a semester, plus a short annual reflection.
